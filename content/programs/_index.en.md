Three connected commitments — money, guidance, and follow-through. Together they
are what allow a student to start, keep going, and finish.

## Scholarship Program {#scholarship}

Direct financial support for school and university fees, books, and exam costs.
We pay the costs that quietly end an education: the term fee that arrives at the
wrong moment, the textbook that never gets bought, the exam entry that goes
unpaid. Support continues year to year as long as a student stays enrolled, so
no one has to reapply for certainty every few months.

## Mentorship {#mentorship}

Money alone is rarely enough. Every student we support is paired with a mentor
who stays with them — someone to talk through subject choices, university
applications, and the practical questions a first-generation student often has
no one else to ask. Mentors are volunteers, matched deliberately, and expected
to stay for the long term rather than a single term.

## Student Progress Review {#progress-review}

We check in regularly on both academic progress and wellbeing. The point is not
to audit anyone — it is to notice early when a student is struggling, so support
can be adjusted while it still matters, rather than after the results arrive.
These reviews also tell us honestly whether our programs are working.
