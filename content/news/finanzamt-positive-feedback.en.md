We are glad to share an encouraging step forward. The tax office (Finanzamt) has
reviewed the constitution (Satzung) of Prarthona i. G. and given us positive
feedback: as drafted, it complies with the legal provisions governing
tax-exempt charitable purposes under German law.

## Why this matters

Before an association can be recognised as tax-privileged in Germany, its
constitution has to satisfy strict requirements — the charitable purpose must be
clearly defined, the use of funds must be restricted to that purpose, and the
rules on what happens to assets if the association is ever dissolved must be
written in a particular way. Getting any of this wrong means going back and
amending the constitution, often after months of delay.

Having the Finanzamt look at our draft and come back positively tells us that
the foundations are sound. It is the difference between hoping we have written
it correctly and having the authority that will ultimately decide say so.

## What it does and does not mean

This feedback is **non-binding**. It is a preliminary assessment of the
constitution, not a decision on our tax status.

- We are **not yet** a registered association, and **not yet** recognised as
  tax-exempt.
- We **cannot** issue donation receipts (Zuwendungsbestätigungen) for tax
  purposes at this stage.
- Formal registration with the local court (Amtsgericht) and recognition of
  tax-privileged status by the Finanzamt are still ahead of us.

We are saying this plainly because we would rather be trusted than impressive.
Anyone considering supporting us should know exactly where we stand.

## What happens next

With the constitution validated, we can proceed to formal registration with the
Amtsgericht. Once entered in the register, we apply to the Finanzamt for
recognition of tax-privileged status. If that is granted, we will be able to
issue donation receipts — and we will update the [Donate](/donate/) page and
post here as soon as that happens.

Our thanks to everyone who helped get the constitution into shape.
